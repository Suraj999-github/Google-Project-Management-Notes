Week-by-week plan

Week 1: Foundations
  • Project vs. operations, life cycle, triple constraint
  • Roles, methodologies, org structures
  • Practice: Q1 to Q20 (Part 1)
Done when: you can name the four phases and explain risk vs. issue.
Week 2: Initiation
  • SMART goals, business case, charter, scope statement
  • Stakeholder register, power-interest grid, RACI
  • Practice: Q21 to Q27, Q61 to Q67
Done when: you have a one-page charter for your own sample project.
Week 3: Planning (schedule)
  • WBS, estimating, dependencies, critical path, slack
  • PERT and three-point formulas
  • Practice: Q28 to Q31, Q68 to Q70
Done when: you can find the critical path in 3 minutes.
Week 4: Planning (budget, risk, communication)
  • Budget, reserves, risk register, EMV
  • Communication plan, procurement basics
  • Practice: Q32 to Q38, Q73 to Q81
Done when: you can build a risk register with 5 scored risks.
Week 5: Execution and quality
  • Monitoring, CV, SV, CPI, SPI, EAC, ETC
  • QA vs. QC, 5 Whys, fishbone, Pareto
  • Practice: Q45 to Q49, Q76 to Q78, Q85 to Q88
Done when: you can do all earned value formulas without notes.
Week 6: Teams, change, closure
  • Tuckman, conflict, feedback, escalation
  • Change control, ADKAR, closure steps
  • Practice: Q39 to Q44, Q50 to Q51, Q89 to Q90, Q99 to Q100
Done when: you can write a closure report outline.
Week 7: Agile
  • Scrum roles, events, artifacts; Kanban and WIP limits
  • User stories, MoSCoW, velocity, burndown
  • Practice: Q52 to Q59, Q91 to Q98
Done when: you can say which practices Scrum requires and which are only common.
Week 8: Capstone, mock tests, career
  • Capstone assignment, two full mock tests, review weak areas
  • LinkedIn, resume bullets, STAR stories, 10 interview answers
Done when: mock scores are at 80% or higher and your profile is updated.

Important notes for a successful certification

Set a steady pace

Study the same hours each week. Coursera lets you set deadlines, so turn them on to stay on track.

Do the hands-on items

Build the documents yourself (charter, RACI, risk register). Do not only watch the videos. These are what you reuse at work.

Learn the formulas cold

CV = EV − AC, SV = EV − PV, CPI = EV ÷ AC, SPI = EV ÷ PV, EAC = BAC ÷ CPI, EMV = probability × impact, PERT = (O + 4M + P) ÷ 6.

Know the confusing pairs

Risk vs. issue, QA vs. QC, severity vs. priority, acceptance criteria vs. Definition of Done, output vs. outcome, crashing vs. fast-tracking.

Read the question wording

"Current performance continues" and "one-off variance" use different EAC formulas. "Select 2" means exactly two. Check units and signs.

Separate required from common (Agile)

Scrum requires the roles, five events, three artifacts and Sprint Goal. Story points, velocity, burndown, user stories and Definition of Ready are common practice.

Terminology can differ

Google's terms, PMBOK terms and company terms are not always the same. For example, "QA" in software often means testing. If unsure, define your term.

Review mistakes, not just scores

Keep an error log. For each miss, write the right answer and why. Retest those topics after three days.

Be honest about credentials

The Google certificate is not the PMP and does not guarantee a job. List it exactly as "Google Project Management Professional Certificate (Coursera)."

Do not memorize leaked answers

Use your own work and original practice. Sharing or using assessment answers breaks course rules and does not build skill.

Confirm official details

Check pass marks, deadlines, fees and course contents on Coursera. They change, so do not rely on memory or this page for them.

Prepare for jobs early

Write one STAR story per week. Turn your technical work into delivery, risk and stakeholder results. Never reveal confidential details.

Study habits that work

Read the learning objectives, decide what you will finish, and remove distractions. Start with 5 minutes of review from the last session.

Pause videos to write a one-line summary. Apply each concept to the BrightCuts example. Mark anything you could not explain out loud.

Do 10 practice questions, log your mistakes, and make 5 flashcards from the hardest ideas.

Do not skip topics. Add one extra week, protect the practice and review time, and cut only optional reading.

Progress tracker

Tick each week when you meet its "done when" goal. Progress resets when you refresh the page.
