## 2. What Is a Project?

A **project** is a temporary effort with a defined start and end, undertaken to create a unique product, service, or result.

| | Project | Operations |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | Temporary | Ongoing |
| Output | Unique | Repetitive |
| Example | Build a booking web app for a salon | Process the salon's daily bookings |
| Ends when | Goals met or project stopped | Business stops the activity |

**Project vs. program vs. portfolio**
- **Project:** one effort with one goal (build the app).
- **Program:** a group of related projects managed together (app + marketing launch + staff training).
- **Portfolio:** all projects and programs an organization runs, chosen to fit strategy (every initiative across the company).

## 3. The Project Management Life Cycle

Google teaches **four phases**:

1. **Initiating:** Why are we doing this? Define goals, scope, stakeholders, and get approval (charter).
2. **Planning:** How will we do it? Schedule, budget, risks, communication, quality.
3. **Executing:** Do the work, lead the team, manage quality and changes, report progress.
4. **Closing:** Confirm delivery, get acceptance, document lessons learned, release the team.

**PMBOK comparison [Supplemental]:** PMI's PMBOK Guide describes **five process groups**: Initiating, Planning, Executing, **Monitoring and Controlling**, Closing. Google folds monitoring into execution. Tracking progress, controlling quality, and managing change run **throughout** the project, not as a separate phase. The seventh edition of PMBOK also moved from process groups to principles and performance domains, so PMI terminology can differ depending on the edition.

**Diagram: where the 20 skills live**

```
INITIATE ──► PLAN ──► EXECUTE ──► CLOSE
   │          │          │          │
   └──────────┴──────────┴──────────┘
     Monitoring, change, communication (continuous)
```

## 4. Essential Terminology

| Term | Plain-English meaning |
|---|---|
| **Stakeholder** | Anyone affected by or able to affect the project |
| **Sponsor** | Senior person who funds, champions, and approves the project |
| **Deliverable** | A tangible or verifiable output (e.g., working login page) |
| **Milestone** | A significant checkpoint with no duration (e.g., "Design approved") |
| **Scope** | The work included (and excluded) in the project |
| **Scope creep** | Uncontrolled additions to scope |
| **Constraint** | A limit on the project (time, cost, scope, quality, resources) |
| **Assumption** | Something believed true but unconfirmed |
| **Dependency** | One task relies on another |
| **Risk** | An uncertain *future* event that could help or hurt |
| **Issue** | A problem that is *already happening* |
| **Charter** | Document formally authorizing the project |
| **Waterfall** | Sequential, plan-first (predictive) approach |
| **Agile** | Iterative, adaptive approach |
| **Backlog** | Prioritized list of work to be done |
| **Change request** | Formal proposal to alter scope, schedule, or budget |
| **Lessons learned** | Documented insights for future projects |

**Triple constraint:** scope, schedule, and cost are linked, so changing one affects the others. Quality sits at the center, and Google also discusses resources and risk as pressures.

**Organizational structures:** *functional* (PM has little authority), *projectized* (PM has high authority), and *matrix* (shared authority, with the team reporting to both a functional manager and the PM).

## 5. How the 20 Skills Fit Together

| Phase | Skills | Connection |
|---|---|---|
| **Initiate** | 1 Project Scoping, 4 Stakeholder Analysis, 5 Scope Management | Scoping defines the boundary, and stakeholder analysis tells you who must agree to it |
| **Plan** | 2 Project Planning, 3 Life Cycle, 6 Risk Management, 7 Stakeholder Communications | The plan turns scope into tasks, time, and cost, while risk and communication plans protect it |
| **Execute** | 8 Implementation, 9 Project Management, 13 Team Management, 14 Team Leadership, 15 Change Management | Implementation is doing the plan, and team skills and change control keep it on course |
| **Quality (throughout)** | 10 Quality Assessment, 11 Quality Management, 12 Quality Assurance | Planned in Planning, checked in Execution, confirmed at Closing |
| **Agile** | 16 Backlogs, 17 Agile Project Management | An alternative way to run planning and execution in short cycles |
| **Close and career** | 18 Project Closure, 19 Web Presence, 20 Interviewing Skills | Closure produces proof of outcomes, which feeds your portfolio and interview stories **[Supplemental]** |

**One thread through everything:** *Scope* defines the work. *Stakeholders* approve it. *Planning* schedules it. *Risk* threatens it. *Communication* aligns people. *Quality* checks it. *Change management* controls revisions. *Closure* proves it.

Our running example is a **web-based booking app for a small business ("BrightCuts Salon")**, which carries through every part.

# 20 Original Practice Questions (Part 1)

*Original practice questions, not official Google assessments.*

---

**Q1 | Project definition | Beginner | MCQ (select 1)**
Which situation is a project?
A. Processing weekly payroll
B. Building a new booking app for a salon
C. Answering customer support emails daily
D. Restocking shelves each morning

**Answer: B.**
**Why:** It has a defined end and creates a unique result. **Why others are wrong:** A, C, and D are repeating operations.
**Concept:** Project vs. operations. **Takeaway:** Ask "does this end, and is the output unique?" before deciding to run something as a project.

---

**Q2 | Project vs. operations | Beginner | True/False**
"Running a company's daily order processing is a project."

**Answer: False.**
**Why:** It's ongoing and repetitive, which makes it operations. A *project* could be building a new order-processing system.
**Concept:** Temporary vs. ongoing work. **Takeaway:** Projects often *change* operations but are not the operations themselves.

---

**Q3 | Project, program, portfolio | Beginner | MCQ (select 1)**
A company manages a mobile app, a website redesign, and an ad campaign together because they share one launch goal. This is a:
A. Portfolio B. Program C. Task list D. Operation

**Answer: B.**
**Why:** Related projects with a shared objective, managed together, form a program. **Why others are wrong:** A portfolio covers *all* initiatives across the organization, not just related ones. C and D don't describe a coordinated set of projects.
**Concept:** Hierarchy of work. **Takeaway:** Know which level you're managing, because reporting and priorities differ.

---

**Q4 | Life cycle | Beginner | MCQ (select 1)**
In Google's four-phase life cycle, which order is correct?
A. Planning, Initiating, Executing, Closing
B. Initiating, Planning, Executing, Closing
C. Initiating, Executing, Planning, Closing
D. Planning, Executing, Closing, Initiating

**Answer: B.**
**Why:** You must justify the project, plan it, do it, then close it. **Why others are wrong:** Each of the others puts a phase before something it depends on.
**Concept:** Life cycle sequence. **Takeaway:** Skipping a phase, especially initiating or planning, is a common cause of later problems.

---

**Q5 | Deliverable vs. milestone | Beginner | MCQ (select 1)**
"Client approves final design" with no work attached is best described as a:
A. Milestone B. Task C. Risk D. Assumption

**Answer: A.**
**Why:** A milestone marks a point in time or a decision and has no duration. **Why others are wrong:** A task has effort and duration. A risk is an uncertain event. An assumption is an unverified belief.
**Concept:** Milestones. **Takeaway:** Use milestones to give stakeholders clear checkpoints.

---

**Q6 | Life cycle | Beginner | True/False**
"Monitoring progress only happens during the Closing phase."

**Answer: False.**
**Why:** Tracking progress, risks, and quality happens continuously, mainly through planning and executing. PMBOK even makes it a process group of its own.
**Concept:** Continuous monitoring. **Takeaway:** Check progress weekly, not only at the end.

---

**Q7 | Triple constraint | Intermediate | Multiple-select (select 3)**
Which three make up the classic triple constraint?
A. Scope B. Schedule C. Cost D. Number of stakeholders E. Office location

**Answer: A, B, C.**
**Why:** They are interdependent, so changing one affects the others. **Why others are wrong:** D and E may influence a project but are not part of the triple constraint.
**Concept:** Constraints. **Takeaway:** When someone asks to change one, explain the impact on the other two.

---

**Q8 | Scope/constraints | Intermediate | Scenario**
Midway through the BrightCuts app, the owner asks for a loyalty-points feature, with no extra budget or time. What should the PM do first?
A. Add it quietly to avoid conflict
B. Refuse immediately
C. Assess the impact on scope, schedule, and cost, then take it through the change process
D. Tell the team to work overtime

**Answer: C.**
**Reasoning:** (1) Treat it as a change request. (2) Estimate effort and impact. (3) Present options to the decision-maker: add budget or time, swap out another feature, or defer. (4) Document the decision. **Why others are wrong:** A is scope creep. B is premature and damages the relationship. D hides the cost instead of managing it.
**Concept:** Change control. **Takeaway:** Never say yes or no before understanding impact.

---

**Q9 | Methodologies | Intermediate | Matching**
Match each approach to its description.
1. Waterfall 2. Scrum 3. Kanban 4. Hybrid
a. Visual board with work-in-progress limits
b. Sequential phases with upfront planning
c. Mix of predictive and agile elements
d. Fixed-length sprints with defined roles

**Answer: 1-b, 2-d, 3-a, 4-c.**
**Why:** Each is defined by its core mechanism: sequence, timeboxed iterations, flow control, or a blend.
**Concept:** Methodologies. **Takeaway:** Choose the approach based on how stable the requirements are.

---

**Q10 | Org structures | Intermediate | MCQ (select 1)**
A project manager has limited authority because team members report mainly to their department heads. This is a:
A. Projectized structure B. Functional structure C. Program office D. Sprint team

**Answer: B.**
**Why:** In a functional structure, authority sits with department managers. **Why others are wrong:** A is the opposite, with PMs holding high authority. C and D are not structure types in this sense.
**Concept:** Authority. **Takeaway:** With low authority, you must influence through relationships and clear agreements.

---

**Q11 | Roles | Beginner | MCQ (select 1)**
Who typically owns and prioritizes the product backlog in Scrum?
A. Project sponsor B. Scrum Master C. Product Owner D. QA engineer

**Answer: C.**
**Why:** The Product Owner maximizes product value and orders the backlog. **Why others are wrong:** The Scrum Master facilitates the process and removes impediments. The sponsor funds but doesn't manage the backlog. A QA engineer is a developer-role contributor.
**Concept:** Scrum roles. **Takeaway:** Don't confuse the PM, Scrum Master, and Product Owner roles.

---

**Q12 | Critical path | Intermediate | Numerical**
Tasks: A (3 days) → B (4 days) → D (1 day). A (3 days) → C (2 days) → D. D can only start after both B and C finish. What is the shortest total project duration?

**Answer: 8 days.**
**Calculation:** Path A-B-D = 3+4+1 = 8. Path A-C-D = 3+2+1 = 6. The longest path sets the minimum duration.
**Plain English:** C has 2 days of slack (float), so it can slip 2 days without delaying the project. B cannot.
**Concept:** Critical path. **Takeaway:** Focus your attention on the longest path.

---

**Q13 | Success criteria | Intermediate | Short answer**
Explain how a project can deliver everything on time and on budget yet still fail.

**Model answer:** If the deliverable doesn't achieve the business outcome (for example, the booking app launches but customers don't use it), the project met its outputs but missed its goals. **Why:** Success criteria should include measurable outcomes, not just delivery.
**Concept:** Outputs vs. outcomes. **Takeaway:** Define measurable goals (e.g., "reduce phone bookings by 40%") at initiation.

---

**Q14 | Predictive vs. adaptive | Intermediate | MCQ (select 1)**
Which project best suits a predictive (waterfall) approach?
A. A startup exploring product features with unclear requirements
B. Constructing a building with fixed regulatory specifications
C. An experimental research app where feedback will change direction
D. A new game prototype

**Answer: B.**
**Why:** Stable, well-understood requirements favor upfront planning. **Why others are wrong:** A, C, and D have uncertain requirements, which favor iterative approaches.
**Concept:** Methodology fit. **Takeaway:** Fit the method to the uncertainty, not to habit.

---

**Q15 | Initiation | Advanced | Scenario**
A sponsor says "Just start the app, we'll sort out the paperwork later." No charter exists. What should the PM do?
A. Start coding immediately
B. Draft a concise charter (goals, scope, stakeholders, success criteria, high-level budget) and get the sponsor's sign-off
C. Wait until the sponsor writes it
D. Skip to the schedule

**Answer: B.**
**Reasoning:** Without a charter, authority and scope are undefined, which is a risk. A short charter respects the sponsor's urgency while protecting the project. **Why others are wrong:** A risks rework and disputes. C is passive. D plans without a foundation.
**Concept:** Project charter. **Takeaway:** Offer a fast, lightweight version rather than refusing.

---

**Q16 | Stakeholders | Beginner | MCQ (select 1)**
Which person is a stakeholder in the BrightCuts app project?
A. The salon owner B. A customer who will book online C. The developer D. All of the above

**Answer: D.**
**Why:** Stakeholders include anyone affected by or influencing the project, whether or not they sit on the team.
**Concept:** Stakeholder identification. **Takeaway:** Include end users early, since they are often forgotten.

---

**Q17 | Initiation documents | Intermediate | Multiple-select (select 2)**
Which two are normally created during initiation?
A. Project charter B. Stakeholder register C. Sprint retrospective notes D. Project closure report

**Answer: A, B.**
**Why:** Both establish authority and identify who is involved. **Why others are wrong:** C belongs to Agile execution. D belongs to Closing.
**Concept:** Phase documents. **Takeaway:** Know which documents belong to which phase.

---

**Q18 | Certification awareness | Intermediate | True/False**
"Completing the Google certificate automatically qualifies you for the PMP credential."

**Answer: False.**
**Why:** The PMP has separate eligibility requirements (education, experience, exam) set by PMI. The Google certificate may help your learning, but it's not an automatic qualification. Check pmi.org for current rules.
**Concept:** Credentials. **Takeaway:** Be accurate about what you hold when writing a resume.

---

**Q19 | Risk vs. issue | Advanced | Scenario**
Statement 1: "The payment vendor might deliver the API late." Statement 2: "The payment vendor has delivered the API two weeks late." Classify each.

**Answer:** 1 is a risk. 2 is an issue.
**Reasoning:** A risk is uncertain and in the future. Once it occurs, it becomes an issue needing action. Risks go in the risk register with a response plan, and issues go in the issue log with an owner and due date.
**Concept:** Risk vs. issue. **Takeaway:** Move items between logs as their status changes.

---

**Q20 | Assumptions vs. constraints | Advanced | MCQ (select 1)**
"The client will provide product photos by March 1" is best categorized as:
A. A constraint B. An assumption C. A deliverable D. A milestone

**Answer: B.**
**Why:** It's believed true but unconfirmed. If untrue, it becomes a risk or an issue. **Why others are wrong:** A constraint is a fixed limit like a deadline. A deliverable is an output. A milestone is a checkpoint.
**Concept:** Assumption log. **Takeaway:** Validate assumptions early and write them down.
